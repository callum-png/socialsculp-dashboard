Bustem Video Review

Scene 7.2–8.1  ·  Frame 9 of 13  ·  March 6, 2026
Overall the direction is solid — the product demo is clear and the loss-to-recovery arc is structurally right. The main issue is that the UI mockup scenes feel too clean and product-demo-ish compared to where the brand actually lives visually. The storyboard was dark, aggressive, confrontational. These scenes feel like a Shopify app pitch. Beyond the visual tone, there are also three key stats from the approved copy that don't appear anywhere in this scene — those are the conversion drivers and they need to be in here.
0:00 – 0:26
Dashboard — Copycat Cards Scroll
Good
Using real brand names — Skims, Temu, Alibaba — is the right call. Viewers recognize them instantly and it makes the product feel real, not hypothetical. Don't lose this.
Good
The "Potential Copycat" label in red and the Approve/Deny buttons make it feel like something is actively happening. It reads as a decision, not just a dashboard.
Fix
The white card backgrounds are killing the brand energy. It looks like a Figma prototype, not a threat detection tool. The whole brand is dark and aggressive — this scene is the opposite.
Try this: Dark card backgrounds — something like #0F0F0F or #111 with a faint border. When a card is flagged as "Potential Copycat," give it a red left-border glow (like a security alert firing). The rest of the card stays dark. That contrast between the neutral cards and the flagged ones will feel like the AI is catching something in real time.
Fix
The cards just appear — there's no sense of the AI scanning and finding. It feels like someone manually scrolled to a list, not a system catching threats live.
Try this: Each card slides in from the right, then after a 0.3s pause, the "Potential Copycat" label flickers on — like a scan completing. A brief red border pulse on the card when it's flagged. It adds maybe 2 seconds total but completely changes the read from "static list" to "live detection."
Consider
A faint red ambient glow in the background that intensifies as more cards get flagged — subtle, but builds tension as the scroll continues. By the time you've seen 5–6 flagged cards, the background should feel slightly alarming.
0:26 – 6:16
Revenue Loss + Shopify Split Screen
Good
The cause-and-effect structure is smart. Copycat on one side, revenue declining on the other — you don't need voiceover to connect the dots. The visual tells the story.
Fix
The negative numbers just appear. They need to hit. Right now -$2,821 / -$7,318 / -$6,653 feel like a spreadsheet. The whole point of this sequence is to make the viewer feel the pain before the payoff.
Try this: Fast counter animation — each number counts down from 0 to its negative value in about 0.4 seconds. On arrival, the number gets a brief red glow and a subtle screen-edge flash (like the screen reacts). Then it settles into place. The count-down makes it feel like money being drained in real time, not a static stat.
Fix
The Shopify sidebar nav is eating too much real estate. It's background context — but right now it's competing with the stats for attention. The ~28% decline badge is nearly invisible at its current size.
Try this: Crop the Shopify view to just the gross sales card — cut off the nav entirely. Slightly blur or darken everything except the number itself. The decline percentage should be 2x its current size and bright red, not a muted orange. It should look like a warning, not a label.
Consider
Between the loss stats, a brief black frame with just the running total in large type — something like "— $16,792 lost so far" — before cutting to the next copycat. Each loss adding to a visible tally would make the sequence feel cumulative and escalating rather than a series of separate examples.
8:08
Recovery Stat — $10,622.98 Gross Sales
Good
Isolating this as a single centered card is exactly right. After all that loss, this frame needs to breathe. The instinct is correct.
Fix
The +28% is the emotional payoff of the whole sequence and it's a footnote. That number is the release — it should feel like a celebration, not a label. It's currently smaller than the gross sales figure it's supposed to amplify.
Try this: Have the card build in sequence — first the gross sales number counts up from $0 (fast, ~0.5s), then the +28% slams in at 2–3x its current size with a green glow burst. Make the percentage the dominant element, not the dollar amount. Optionally: add a brief upward tick animation on the trend line right as the percentage appears.
Alternative
Consider a split-frame cut right before this scene: the previous loss number (-$6,653, still in red) fades out on the left half of the screen as the recovery card builds in on the right. A literal before/after in a single frame. Reinforces the contrast without needing narration.
13:15
Founder CTA — "Get Ur Scan Below"
Good
The neon Bustem sign is genuinely great production value. It makes the whole thing feel legit and ties directly to the brand identity. Keep it prominent.
Fix
"GET UR SCAN BELOW" looks like a default lower-third. It's the last thing the viewer sees and it has almost no visual weight. The copy is also weaker than what you have — "world's first FREE AI threat scan" is a far more compelling line.
Try this: Replace it entirely. Before cutting to the founder, do a brief full-screen text moment: black background, then "WORLD'S FIRST FREE AI THREAT SCAN" stamped in Anton font, large, white — one word appearing at a time with a quick flash between each word (like a security clearance screen). Then cut to the founder. The CTA has already landed before he even opens his mouth.
Alternative
If you want to keep the lower-third format: use Anton font at 2x the current size, add a blue glow matching the neon sign behind it, and change the copy to "FREE AI THREAT SCAN — LINK BELOW." Short, high-contrast, brand-matched. The neon already gives you the ambient light — lean into it.
Consider
The founder's shirt says "GET UR SCAN BELOW" which is fine, but if the on-screen text says the same thing it's redundant. Let the shirt handle the direct CTA instruction and let the text overlay carry the offer — "WORLD'S FIRST FREE AI THREAT SCAN." Two elements, two jobs.
Missing — Not in Any Frame

These three lines from the approved copy don't appear anywhere in this scene. They're the conversion drivers — the video needs them.

43 counterfeits eliminated per month
Should appear as a text overlay during the dashboard scroll — bold white on dark, bottom-left corner. It contextualizes the volume. Viewers need to grasp the scale before they see individual examples.
$240,000 in lost revenue recovered
Needs an interstitial frame between the loss sequence and the recovery card. This is the macro stat that makes the $10K feel achievable for the viewer. Without it, the arc goes from individual losses straight to one win — the cumulative scale is missing.
World's first FREE AI threat scan
This is the hook. It deserves a dedicated full-screen moment — not a lower-third afterthought. Ideally lands right before the founder shot so the offer is planted in the viewer's head before the founder reinforces it.
What to Fix First
1

Add the 3 missing copy elements

43/month overlay on Scene A, $240K interstitial before the recovery card, and a dedicated free scan frame. These are the stats that make someone think "that's my problem." Without them the video is a demo reel, not a conversion ad.

2

Dark card backgrounds + red flagging on Scene A

Switch card backgrounds from white to dark, add red border glow on flagged items, slide-in animation with a 0.3s flag delay. This single change fixes the brand tone mismatch for the whole scene.

3

Counter animation on the loss numbers

Count down from 0 to negative in 0.4s with a red glow on arrival. Crop out the Shopify nav, make the decline % bigger and red. The pain has to be felt before the recovery lands.

4

Full-screen CTA frame + upgrade the text

Stamped full-screen "WORLD'S FIRST FREE AI THREAT SCAN" in Anton before the founder shot. Use the strong copy line, not "get ur scan below." Last impression — treat it like one.
